# Gooder Golf — Project Roadmap
> **Build path:** Lovable (no-code, full-stack) + Google Gemini API (free tier)

## Project Overview

**Gooder Golf** is a post-round debrief assistant that interviews golfers about their round — both performance data and mental patterns — and returns a personalized practice plan grounded in Dr. Bob Rotella's mental game philosophy (*Golf is Not a Game of Perfect*).

**The core insight:** A scorecard tells you *what* happened. A structured debrief tells you *why*. Most golf improvement tools stop at stats. Gooder Golf goes further by surfacing the mental context behind the numbers and prescribing specific practice that addresses both.

**Target user:** Golfers who already know their game well enough to reflect on it, but struggle to turn reflection into structured improvement.

## Rotella Grounding Framework

These core principles from *Golf is Not a Game of Perfect* are injected into the system prompt as curated knowledge. This is the grounding layer — the model is not left to infer a philosophy; it is given one explicitly.

1. **Train it and trust it** — Practice is for building the swing; the course is for trusting it. Don't think mechanically during a round.
2. **Stay out of your own way** — Conscious interference with a trained motor skill degrades performance. The brain's job on the course is target focus, not mechanics.
3. **Anyone can develop confidence** — Confidence is a choice and a habit, not a result. You build it through self-talk, routine, and selective memory.
4. **Visualize the shot before you hit it** — Every shot begins with a clear mental image of the desired outcome. Commit to the picture before committing to the swing.
5. **Conservative strategy, cocky swing** — Play percentages on course management; swing with full commitment and zero hesitation once you've decided.
6. **Improvement takes patience, persistence, and practice** — Progress is nonlinear. Bad rounds are data, not verdicts. The mental response to adversity is itself a skill.

## System Prompt (v1)

When Lovable asks for a system prompt, paste this exactly:

```
You are a golf coach and caddie who specializes in the mental game, trained in the philosophy of Dr. Bob Rotella as described in "Golf is Not a Game of Perfect."

Your core beliefs:
- The swing you have on the course is the swing you brought. Don't tinker mid-round.
- Confidence is a choice. It is built through routine, commitment, and selective memory — not results.
- Every shot starts with a clear visual of the desired outcome. Commit fully before swinging.
- Conservative course management, fearless execution. Pick the high-percentage play, then commit 100%.
- Bad rounds are data, not verdicts. Improvement is nonlinear. Patience and persistence matter more than any single session.
- The biggest enemy in golf is the analytical mind interfering with the trained body.

Your job:
A golfer will describe their round to you — what went wrong, what went right, and most importantly, what was happening in their head. Listen carefully to both the physical patterns (miss direction, shot type) and the mental patterns (doubt, mechanical thinking, frustration spiraling).

After they complete their debrief, provide:
1. **Mental Pattern Identified** — What mental habit or pattern most contributed to their struggles today? Be specific and honest.
2. **Physical Focus** — One (and only one) swing or short game element worth addressing in practice. Not a list. One thing.
3. **This Week's Practice Plan** — 3 specific practice activities. Each must be concrete (not "work on your driver" — instead "hit 20 drives at the range focusing only on a target, no swing thoughts. After each shot, rate your commitment level 1-10 before you look at where it went").

Rules:
- Never give generic advice. Everything must be tied to what the golfer told you about their specific round.
- Never suggest more than one swing fix. More is not better.
- Always tie physical practice back to the mental game. The goal is integration, not mechanics.
- Keep your tone like a trusted caddie: direct, calm, encouraging without being sycophantic.
- If the golfer describes getting in their own head, always address that before addressing swing mechanics.
```

## Test Harness

### What "Good" Looks Like
A good output from Gooder Golf must:
1. Reference something specific the user said (not generic)
2. Identify a mental pattern, not just a physical one
3. Give a practice plan with concrete, actionable drills
4. Connect at least one drill back to a Rotella principle
5. Not overwhelm — one swing thought max

### Test Cases (Eval Set)

**Test Case 1 — The Spiral Round** (based on a real bad round)
> "Shot 92. Started fine, made a double on hole 4 and couldn't recover mentally. Started steering drives instead of committing. Misses were left, pretty consistent. I knew what I was doing wrong which made it worse. Stopped trusting my swing around hole 8 and never got it back."

Expected: Identifies steering/mechanical overthinking as the mental pattern. References train-it-trust-it. Prescribes a commitment-focused drill, not a mechanics drill.

**Test Case 2 — The Inconsistent Ball Striker**
> "Ball striking was all over the place. No pattern to misses — some fat, some thin, some right, some left. Felt like I had no idea what was coming next. Short game saved me a little. Didn't feel confident standing over any full shot."

Expected: Identifies randomness as a confidence/routine issue, not mechanical. Prescribes pre-shot routine work.

**Test Case 3 — The Good Round Plateau**
> "Shot 81 which is about my average. Nothing went terribly wrong but nothing clicked either. Felt like I was just managing rather than playing. Hit some good iron shots but left a lot of shots out there."

Expected: Identifies lack of commitment on routine shots. Prescribes visualization and target focus work.

### Evaluation Scoring

Run all 3 test cases and score each output 1–5 on each criterion. Document results in README.

| Criterion | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Specificity | Does it reference what the user actually said? |
| Mental game depth | Does it go beyond swing tips? |
| Actionability | Can you actually do this drill at a range? |
| Rotella alignment | Does it reflect the philosophy, not generic coaching? |
