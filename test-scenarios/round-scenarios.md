# Golf Round Test Scenarios

These scenarios represent realistic golfer debriefs to evaluate the app's coaching recommendations. Each includes answers to the four debrief questions: score vs normal game, bad shot patterns, thoughts on worst shots, and mental response after bad shots.

---

## Scenario 1: The Mental Spiral
**Golfer Profile:** Mid-handicap, plays 2-3x per week, knows their swing but struggles with consistency

**Score vs Normal Game:**
"Started 2-over, had a great front nine. Back nine was a complete meltdown—finished 6-over. Lost 4 shots in the last three holes."

**Bad Shot Patterns / Where They Happened:**
"Three-putts on holes 10 and 12. Duffed a chip on 17. Completely topped a drive on 18. Everything went wrong on the back nine after I bogeyed 11."

**Thoughts on Worst Shots:**
"On the three-putt at 10, I was thinking about how I needed to make the putt to stay under par. That got in my head. By 17, I was so frustrated I wasn't even thinking anymore—just reacting. The duff surprised me. On 18, I was just trying to hit it hard to make up for the bad shots, swung way too fast."

**Mental Response After Bad Shots:**
"That's where I fell apart. After the bogey on 11, I got angry at myself. Told myself I was playing stupid. By 17, I was past angry—I was just defeated. Didn't recover mentally at all."

## Scenario 2: The Tension Shot
**Golfer Profile:** Single-digit handicap, competitive, pressure sensitive

**Score vs Normal Game:**
"Shot 1-over. Should've been even, maybe better. Lost it off the tee today."

**Bad Shot Patterns / Where They Happened:**
"Hook on hole 4 into trees—lost the ball. Slice on hole 9 OB. Both with driver. My irons were solid. It was purely off the tee."

**Thoughts on Worst Shots:**
"On 4, I was thinking about the narrow fairway. Tried to steer it. On 9, I made a swing thought about staying down through the ball, but that made me tense. Both times I felt my shoulders tight before I even swung."

**Mental Response After Bad Shots:**
"I recovered okay mentally. Told myself it happens and moved on. But the damage was done—those two shots cost me the round."

## Scenario 3: The Physical Meltdown
**Golfer Profile:** Newer golfer (1-2 years), improving, inconsistent

**Score vs Normal Game:**
"Shot 92. That's about normal for me, but it could've been 85. Wasted so many strokes around the green."

**Bad Shot Patterns / Where They Happened:**
"Chunked three chips. Thin one on 7, fat ones on 13 and 16. All within 30 yards of the green. Missed three short putts, all in the 4-foot range."

**Thoughts on Worst Shots:**
"Honestly, I was thinking too much. On the chips, I was trying to remember the technique—keep my hands forward, hit down, all that. On the putts, I was worried about the read instead of just hitting the putt."

**Mental Response After Bad Shots:**
"I got frustrated. Said a few bad words after each one. But I didn't throw a club or anything. Just annoyed with myself."

## Scenario 4: The One Bad Hole
**Golfer Profile:** Consistent, good ball striker, occasional mental lapses

**Score vs Normal Game:**
"Shot 79, which is right where I normally am. But hole 8 was a disaster—made a 9 on a par 4. That was the only hole that derailed me."

**Bad Shot Patterns / Where They Happened:**
"Just the one hole. Drove it in the rough, tried to hit it over water and short-sided myself, then chipped into the bunker twice. Should've laid up."

**Thoughts on Worst Shots:**
"I knew I should lay up but didn't want to accept the penalty. Kept thinking I could pull off the hero shot. Stubbornness, honestly. After the first bad shot, I just kept digging the hole deeper."

**Mental Response After Bad Shots:**
"After the third bad shot in the sequence, I was frustrated but I reset. Took a breath, accepted it, and played well the rest of the way. The back nine was solid."

## Scenario 5: The Short Game Killer
**Golfer Profile:** Good driver and iron player, weak short game

**Score vs Normal Game:**
"Shot 88, two worse than my average. Drove and hit irons great—hit maybe 12 greens. But made only 5 birdies when I should've made 10."

**Bad Shot Patterns / Where They Happened:**
"Every time I had a chip or pitch inside 40 yards, something went wrong. Either I was too aggressive with distance or left it short. Same issue with lag putting—couldn't control distance from 40+ feet."

**Thoughts on Worst Shots:**
"I wasn't really thinking, that's the problem. I'd just see the shot and execute without a clear plan. No target distance, no visualization of the landing spot. Just hit and hope."

**Mental Response After Bad Shots:**
"I accepted it and moved on. No frustration, but also no strategy to fix it. Just kept happening hole after hole."

## Scenario 6: The Grip It and Rip It
**Golfer Profile:** Newer golfer, athletic but impatient

**Score vs Normal Game:**
"Shot 96. Solid for me, but I hit some really good shots and some really stupid ones."

**Bad Shot Patterns / Where They Happened:**
"Pulled driver left on 2, 5, and 14. Three holes where I felt strong and just swung hard. Also had three other bad shots where I just didn't have a good pre-shot routine—hit before I was ready."

**Thoughts on Worst Shots:**
"Before the pulled drives, I was thinking 'let's crush it.' No focus. On the other shots, I honestly wasn't thinking about anything specific. Just walked up and hit."

**Mental Response After Bad Shots:**
"Got frustrated with myself for not staying focused. Told myself to be more deliberate, but then I'd do it again three holes later."

## Scenario 7: The Wind Day
**Golfer Profile:** Experienced golfer, adaptable, course management

**Score vs Normal Game:**
"Shot 76, two better than usual. Windy day and most people struggled, but I played smart."

**Bad Shot Patterns / Where They Happened:**
"Only real mistake was hole 11—hit 6-iron when I should've hit 5. Missed the green left. One bad decision."

**Thoughts on Worst Shots:**
"I thought I could keep it in with the 6, but the wind was tricky. Overestimated my ability to hold the line. Should've trusted my first instinct to go with the stronger club."

**Mental Response After Bad Shots:**
"Accepted it immediately. Moved on. It was one shot in a good round—no reason to dwell."

---

## Scenario 8: The Comeback
**Golfer Profile:** Mid-to-high handicap, mentally resilient despite technical struggles

**Score vs Normal Game:**
"Shot 98. Started terribly—was 12-over after nine. Shot 40 on the front. Came back and shot 58 on the back. Much better."

**Bad Shot Patterns / Where They Happened:**
"Front nine: couldn't find the fairway (4 drives in rough), rushed my shots, no rhythm. Back nine was different—I slowed down and committed to each shot."

**Thoughts on Worst Shots:**
"On the front, I was in a rush. Playing fast, not thinking. After the turn, I told myself this is still my round and I can salvage it. Started picking targets, taking my time. Same swing, different mindset."

**Mental Response After Bad Shots:**
"Front nine: angry, frustrated, thinking I was playing badly and couldn't fix it. Back nine: I just accepted each shot and moved to the next one. Stopped trying to fix my swing and started trusting it."

## Evaluation Notes

Use these scenarios to test:

1. **Mental Pattern Identification:** Does the coach correctly identify whether the issue is mental (tension, overthinking, overconfidence) vs. physical (technique)?
2. **Specificity:** Are recommendations specific to the debrief, or generic?
3. **Prioritization:** Does it address mental issues first when present?
4. **Physical Focus:** Is there exactly ONE actionable item, tied to the mental game?
5. **Practice Plan:** Are the three activities concrete and achievable in a week?
6. **Tone:** Does it sound like a trusted caddie—direct but encouraging?
7. **Knowledge Base Reference:** Does it reference relevant quotes or principles?
